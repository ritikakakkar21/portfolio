Six EU Markets, One Migration Window

01

Six markets, one window

Sportstech operates separate country properties across the EU. Migrating them meant moving six markets more or less concurrently, each with its own URL patterns, language variants and accumulated authority.

The risk profile is unforgiving: a migration failure does not degrade gracefully, it deletes visibility. Everything that protected this launch happened before it.

02

The pre-migration playbook

  1. Full URL inventory audit

    Catalogued every indexable URL per market, with traffic, rankings and inbound links attached, so nothing valuable could disappear silently.

  2. 1:1 redirect mapping

    Mapped old to new at a one-to-one level rather than defaulting to category-level redirects — the difference between preserving equity and pooling it into irrelevance.

  3. hreflang validation

    Validated the full hreflang matrix across six markets and language variants to keep the right page in front of the right country.

  4. Indexation and equity checks

    Ran indexation checks and a link-equity preservation strategy through launch, monitoring Search Console per property.

03

The rich snippet bug

In parallel, I rolled out AggregateRating JSON-LD across the full product catalog, integrating review data from Reviews.io and Trustpilot to make star ratings eligible in search results.

The initial rollout did not surface. Rather than accept it, I diagnosed a markup and rendering issue that was blocking rich-snippet eligibility — and fixed it site-wide, restoring star ratings across the catalog.

Why it matters

Star ratings lift click-through on commercial queries without any ranking change. Recovering them across a full catalog is one of the highest-leverage technical wins available in e-commerce SEO.

04

Post-migration hygiene

After launch, I ran recurring Screaming Frog audits across the DE, FR and wider EU properties to catch regressions early.

  • Crawl errors and broken redirect chains.
  • Core Web Vitals across templates and markets.
  • Canonical conflicts introduced by localisation.
  • Structured data gaps and XML sitemap errors.

Results

A migration judged by what did not happen: no visibility collapse.
