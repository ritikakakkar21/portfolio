Case 01  /  Sportstech

AI SEO + Programmatic Growth System

Growing an EU e-commerce brand inside a hard content freeze — by building a system instead of a content calendar.

01

The constraint that shaped everything

Sportstech is a fitness equipment brand selling across Germany, France and the wider EU. When I took over search, the growth target was aggressive — but the operating reality was not: strict content constraints meant almost no scope to create or publish new pages.

That removes the default SEO answer (publish more, publish faster) and leaves only the harder one: make what already exists retrievable, and build a system that scales inside the guardrails. At the same time, an increasing share of buying research had moved to AI surfaces — AI Overviews, ChatGPT, Perplexity — where extraction quality matters more than word count.

The real problem

Traditional SEO expansion was blocked, and existing content was not structured for how large language models select, quote and attribute sources.

02

What the audit actually showed

Before touching anything, I mapped the catalog against real query demand and against how answer engines were citing competitors.

  • Thin entity coverage. Product and category pages described features but never covered the entities, attributes and comparisons that models need to answer a question.
  • Answers buried in prose. The information existed, but not in an extractable shape — no direct answers, no structured blocks, weak semantic hierarchy.
  • Unserved high-intent long tail. Hundreds of specific, commercially valuable queries had no dedicated destination.
  • Low AI visibility. Tracking in seoClarity and Profound showed the brand was largely absent from answer-engine citations in its own category.
  • Manual, repetitive workflows. Clustering, metadata and internal linking were all hand-built, so every improvement cost hours that could not scale.
03

The bet: build a system, not a content calendar

I designed a programmatic growth system with four layers — each one built to keep working without me in the loop.

  1. A programmatic SEO framework

    Built high-intent keyword clusters and long-tail query maps into a repeatable page framework, so scale came from templates and data rather than from ad-hoc briefs.

  2. An AI + automation layer

    Wired ChatGPT and n8n into the workflow to handle keyword clustering, content generation, metadata and internal linking — the repetitive work that used to gate every release.

  3. LLM and semantic optimization

    Rebuilt pages around entity coverage, contextual relevance and answer-engine formatting so models could lift a clean, attributable answer.

  4. Full-funnel intent mapping

    Aligned every cluster to awareness, consideration or conversion intent, so the system grew qualified traffic instead of vanity sessions.

04

How it shipped

Execution ran in weekly loops. The automation produced candidate pages and metadata; I reviewed for accuracy, brand voice and semantic completeness; the internal-linking routine then wired new pages into the existing topic graph so authority flowed to commercial pages.

  • High-volume, intent-driven pages published through the template system — within the brand's content constraints.
  • Continuous optimization cycles on click-through rate, engagement and rankings rather than one-off launches.
  • Internal linking treated as a first-class deliverable to compound topical authority.
  • AI visibility tracked as its own KPI in seoClarity and Profound, next to classic organic reporting.

The takeaway

Scaling SEO in 2026 is not a volume problem, it is a systems problem. Design for how AI retrieves answers, then let automation carry the repetition.
