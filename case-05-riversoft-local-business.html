Case 05  /  Riversoft

Local Business: 77 to 781 Clicks in Four Months

A near-invisible local site rebuilt in the right order — fundamentals first, then authority, then conversion.

01

Starting from almost zero

Riversoft came in with 77 clicks and roughly 3,000 impressions a month — effectively invisible. There was no keyword footprint, no local presence, thin service content and no internal structure to help either users or crawlers.

With a small site and a small budget, sequencing mattered more than tactics. Fundamentals first, then authority, then conversion.

02

Phase one: fundamentals

  1. Keyword research

    Identified high-traffic, low-competition terms the site could realistically win, mapped to the services that actually generate revenue.

  2. On-page optimization

    Rewrote titles, meta descriptions and URL structures for relevance and click-through, and restructured headers around search intent.

  3. Content creation and expansion

    Filled the gaps with in-depth, intent-driven pages instead of thin service stubs, answering the questions prospects actually ask.

03

Phase two: structure, speed and authority

  • Internal linking to spread authority and give crawlers a clear path through the site.
  • Site speed and mobile responsiveness improvements to protect rankings and reduce drop-off.
  • Backlink acquisition through outreach and guest blogging to build domain trust from a standing start.
  • Google Business Profile optimization plus localized content to capture nearby, high-intent demand.
04

Phase three: readability and conversion

Traffic only counts if it converts. I rewrote pages for scannability — shorter paragraphs, bullets, clear formatting — optimized them to answer search intent directly, and placed strong CTAs on every service page.

Search Console was monitored continuously across the four months so the plan could shift towards whatever was compounding fastest.

The takeaway

For a small site, compounding beats cleverness. Fix the fundamentals in the right order and the curve takes care of itself.
